Mastering the Board: The Blueprint for an Unbeatable Gomoku AI

Welcome to Gomoku, the classic strategy board game. Also known as Five-in-a-Row, the goal is simple: be the first player to form an unbroken chain of five of your stones horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. This guide provides the full **architectural blueprint** for building a modern, unbeatable Gomoku application, covering everything from basic rules to advanced AI (Minimax and pattern matching).

How to Play (Quick Start)

  • Players: Two players (Black and White). Black plays first.
  • The Board: Played on a 15x15 grid.
  • Placing Stones: Players take turns placing one of their stones on any empty intersection.
  • Winning: The first player to get **five** of their stones in a continuous line wins the game.

1. GAME INFO: The Essence of Gomoku Logic

Gomoku's logic revolves around a single condition: achieving five sequential stones. However, the 15x15 board offers over 100 billion possible combinations, making brute-force checks impossible. The power of our logic lies in efficient pattern detection and move prioritization.

1.1. Logic & Core Architecture

The game's intelligence is compartmentalized into a pure logic layer (Conceptually, `GomokuLogic`). This module manages the 15x15 array state and handles two primary tasks: State Management (tracking 225 potential moves) and Win Detection. The separation of this core logic from the user interface is crucial for building a testable and maintainable game.

  • State Management: Tracks the 15x15 board state and alternates the current player (Black starts, then White).
  • Win Detection: Efficiently scans around the last placed stone to detect any 5-in-a-row horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.

2. The AI Challenge: Minimax and Pattern Scoring

Developing an unbeatable AI for a game with a search space as vast as Gomoku is challenging. Standard Minimax fails quickly due to the massive branching factor (up to 225 possible moves per turn).

2.1. The Minimax Core with Alpha-Beta Pruning

The AI relies on the **Minimax algorithm** with **Alpha-Beta Pruning** to efficiently explore the game tree. Due to the board size, the search depth must be limited unless key optimizations like Move Ordering (only checking moves adjacent to existing stones) and Transposition Tables (caching board states) are implemented.

2.2. Pattern Recognition and Evaluation Function

The AI's brain relies entirely on recognizing and scoring key patterns:

  • **Live Four:** Four stones in a row with open ends on both sides. This is an immediate, forced win (score = infinity).
  • **Live Three (Open Three):** Three stones in a row with open ends, creating a potential 'Live Four' on the next turn.
  • **Forks (Double Threats):** Creating two or more simultaneous threats (e.g., two Live Threes), forcing the opponent to block one while the other matures.

3. Platform and Technology Stack

This Gomoku application is a single-file, zero-dependency masterpiece, maximizing efficiency and portability while delivering a complete experience:

  • **HTML5 Structure:** Provides the semantic backbone and the 225 intersection elements.
  • **CSS Grid:** Essential for responsive layout, ensuring the 15x15 board scales perfectly on any device.
  • **Vanilla JavaScript (ES6+):** Handles all game logic, turn switching, and the eventual AI integration.
  • **Classic Traditional Aesthetics:** Maintains the high-contrast yellow board and black/white stones for an authentic experience.

4. Advanced Gomoku Strategy

To defeat a powerful AI or a skilled human opponent, you must master the art of the fork attack.

4.1. The Importance of the Center

The very center intersection (H8) is the most valuable square. **Black must take the center on the first move.** Subsequent moves should expand control outward from the center, using diagonals to isolate the opponent.

4.2. Mastering the Double Three (The Fork)

The most common winning pattern is the Double Three. By creating two non-connected "Live Threes" simultaneously, you create an unstoppable threat. Your opponent can only block one of the threes, allowing the other to be extended into a forced win.

5. FAQ About Gomoku

Q: Why is the AI implementation so much harder for Gomoku than for Connect 4?

A: Gomoku lacks gravity and has a massive 15x15 search space. The lack of gravity forces the AI to check the entire board globally, increasing computational demands dramatically compared to the smaller, constrained 6x7 Connect 4 board.

Q: What is a "Live Three"?

A: A Live Three is three stones in a row with empty spaces on both ends. This is the critical pattern needed to set up a Live Four or a winning fork.
